Got a cold, gray day and kids climbing the walls? Pull five dried herbs out of the cupboard. Your kids close their eyes, smell each one, make a guess, and write down what happens. That's a real science lesson, and it takes about twenty minutes.

## Set up the test

Put a spoonful of each herb in its own small bowl or cup. Five is a good number:

- peppermint leaf
- rosemary leaf
- lavender flowers
- chamomile flowers
- cinnamon chips

Write each name on a sticky note and put it under the bowl, so nobody can peek. Number the bowls 1 to 5.

## Guess first, then test

Before anyone smells a thing, have each child write down (or tell you) a guess: How many of the five do you think you'll get right?

That's a hypothesis. It's the first step of real science, and kids love having something to prove.

Then eyes closed. Hold each bowl under their nose, one at a time, and let them name it. Write their answer next to the bowl number. Flip the sticky notes and count up the score.

## Describe what you smell

Now let them smell each one again with their eyes open, and find words for it. Sweet? Sharp? Cool? Woody? Like a candle, like a cookie, like the yard after you mow?

Ask: Which two smell the most alike? Which one did everybody get right? Which one fooled you?

## Sort them by plant part

Older kids can take it one step further. Every one of these came from a different part of a plant:

- Peppermint and rosemary are leaves.
- Lavender and chamomile are flowers.
- Cinnamon is bark.

Look at each one up close. The rosemary leaves are curled like little needles. The lavender is a pile of tiny buds. Chamomile still has its little yellow centers. Have your kids draw one from each group and label it.

## Run it again next month

Save the herbs in jars and run the test again in a few weeks. Did the scores go up? Why? That's a second experiment, and it's a great one to write down.

## Every smell was His idea

"The earth brought forth vegetation, plants yielding seed after their kind, and trees bearing fruit with seed in them, after their kind; and God saw that it was good" (Genesis 1:12, NASB1995). Every one of those five smells was His idea before it was ever in a jar. That's worth saying out loud at the end of the lesson.

## A quick safety note

Keep this one to smelling and looking. Chamomile is in the daisy family, so skip it if your child is sensitive to ragweed or daisies.
