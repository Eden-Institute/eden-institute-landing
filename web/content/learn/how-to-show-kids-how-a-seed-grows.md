You don't need a garden or a sunny day for this one. You need a dried bean, a glass jar, a paper towel and about a week. Your kids get to see the inside of a seed, and then they get to watch one come to life on your kitchen counter.

## Start by opening a seed

Put a few big dried beans in a bowl of water before bed. Lima beans are perfect because they're large and flat. In the morning the skin slips right off and the bean splits into two halves.

Now look closely along the inside edge of one half. There's a tiny plant tucked in there, with what look like two little folded leaves. That's the beginning of the whole plant, packed up and waiting. The two big halves around it are its lunchbox: stored food to keep the baby plant going until it has leaves of its own.

Hand your kids a magnifying glass and let them find it themselves. Then ask: What do you think this part will become? Why does a seed need to carry its own food?

## Grow one where your kids can watch the roots

Take a clear glass jar and line the inside with a damp paper towel. Slide two or three beans between the paper towel and the glass, about halfway down, so you can see them from the outside. Pour a little water in the bottom so the towel stays wet.

Then wait. In a few days to a week, depending on how warm your kitchen is, the root comes out first and heads down. After that a little stem pushes up. Kids love that the root always knows which way is down.

Want to see it for yourself? Once the root is growing, turn the jar on its side. Over the next day or two, watch the root bend and start growing down again.

## Let them draw it every day

This is the part that turns a fun project into real science. Give each child a notebook and have them draw the bean once a day, with the date at the top. They'll notice things you never pointed out: the root getting fuzzy, the stem hooking over before it straightens, the two halves shrinking as the plant uses up its food.

Looking closely and drawing what you see is how kids learn to pay attention to creation. It's the heart of how I wrote [Eden's Table](/homeschool). In Sprouts, the K-2 year, your child holds one real plant each week, smells it, draws it in their notebook and says its name.

## The part only God does

Your kids can soak the bean, wet the towel and check the jar every morning. They still can't make it grow. Scripture says it plainly: "I planted, Apollos watered, but God was causing the growth" (1 Corinthians 3:6, NASB1995). And Jesus described it the same way your kids will see it in the jar: "first the blade, then the head, then the mature grain in the head" (Mark 4:28, NASB1995).

That's a good thing to talk about at the kitchen table while you wait.
