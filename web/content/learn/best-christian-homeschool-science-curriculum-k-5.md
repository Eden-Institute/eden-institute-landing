When a mom asks me which science curriculum to use for her K-5 kids, I tell her to put the cover down and read a full week of lessons. You're looking for Scripture inside the lessons, a plan that fits your real days and your kids' ages, and a sample you can actually teach before you pay for anything.

I taught for twelve years, and one of the curricula below is the one I wrote, Eden's Table. You should know that before you read any further. So this guide sticks to what each program is like, with links so you can see it for yourself.

## How do I choose a Christian homeschool science curriculum?

These are the questions I'd work through with any program, mine included.

- **How much prep does it ask of me?** Be honest about the time you actually have, not the time you wish you had. Check whether the lessons are scripted, whether the discussion questions are written out, and whether there's a supply list for the week. If not, you'll be building those yourself the night before.
- **Does it fit how my kids learn?** A five-year-old learns a plant with her hands and her nose. An older child is ready to measure, record and test an idea. Some programs are built around a parent reading a book aloud, and others hand the lesson to a short video.
- **Can my children share it?** If you teach more than one child, look at whether siblings can work the same lesson at different levels, or whether you'd be teaching two separate programs.
- **Does each lesson credit the thing made, or the One who made it?** This is the test I run on every line I write. A science lesson can drift away from the Creator without anyone meaning it to, so read a page in the middle of the year, not just the introduction.

There's more on each of these in [How to Evaluate a Christian Homeschool Curriculum](/learn/how-to-evaluate-christian-homeschool-curriculum).

## What are the most popular Christian homeschool science programs?

These are the names that come up again and again in Christian homeschool science lists and reviews.

**Apologia, Exploring Creation (Young Explorer series), grades K-6.** A parent reads the book aloud, and each book goes deep on one or two topics, like botany or astronomy, for a whole year. Notebooking journals go with each book. It leans on Charlotte Mason ideas like narration and nature study.

**Master Books.** *Let's Talk Science* covers K-4 with everything for a level in one book. *God's Design* is written for several ages at once across grades 1-8, with a Beginners level for the youngest.

**Science Shepherd, grades 1-5.** Short daily video lessons with a workbook, in two levels (Level A for grades 1-3, Level B for grades 3-5).

**Berean Builders, Science in History series.** Written for several elementary ages together. It teaches science in the order it was discovered, with a hands-on experiment in each lesson.

**BJU Press and Abeka, grades 1-6.** Grade-by-grade science textbooks with teacher guides, closest to a traditional school year.

## Where does nature study fit?

Charlotte Mason put nature study and time outdoors first for young children. It means your child learns about God's creation from the real thing: a plant in their hand, a drawing in their own nature notebook, and telling back what they noticed in their own words. You don't need a botany degree or a shelf of field guides. You need a plant, a pencil and a little unhurried attention. I walk through a full week of it in [Charlotte Mason Nature Study](/learn/charlotte-mason-nature-study).

## Where does Eden's Table fit?

Eden's Table is plant science, one plant a week. Why plants? Because a plant is science a child can hold. Botany, plant identification and careful observation are real science, and a young child can do all three with a leaf in her hand. She rolls a dried bud between two fingers, smells it, feels the leaves, then draws the plant and labels its parts. That habit of looking closely before naming anything is what every other science depends on.

Each week also has a kitchen lab, where your child measures, observes and records. In Seedlings, color, smell, taste and texture turn into measurements, ratios and a hypothesis your child writes and then tests. Sprouts is written for K-2 and Seedlings for 3-5, and each is a full 36-week year.

You don't have to be a science person to teach it. The Teacher's Guide writes out every lesson, and the week's kitchen lab supplies are listed for you. Most days are written at two depths, a line for younger children and one for older, so they can work the same week at one table. Same lesson, same table.

And every plant points back to the One who made it. "How many are Your works! In wisdom You have made them all" (Psalm 104:24, NASB).

## Can I use it alongside another science program?

Yes. Use Eden's Table as your science and nature study, or keep what's working this year and add it alongside. One plant a week brings a lot with it. The Week at a Glance page shows which day carries Bible, science, language arts, math, art, history, geography, Latin, health and character, so you can see right away where it overlaps with what you already teach and where it fills a gap.

So a family reading one of Apologia's books this year can keep it and add Eden's Table for plants and nature study.

## Is Eden's Table a complete science curriculum?

It's plant science. There is no physics or earth science strand. So you can use it as your science and nature study, or add it alongside what's already working.
