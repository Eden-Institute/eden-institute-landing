Science is all about exploration and figuring out why everything works the way it does. So when a mom asks me how to teach science to her kindergartner, my answer is simple: let discovery lead.

## Start with the questions your child is already asking

What does your child ask about when they're going a million miles an hour and firing off a whole string of questions? That's what you dig into. You feed that curiosity.

Kids wonder about things in nature all the time. It's built in. It doesn't have to be taught. Your job isn't to create curiosity in a five-year-old. It's to notice it and give it somewhere to go.

That's how you raise a lifelong learner.

## Why curiosity matters more than covering topics

The goal at this age isn't to finish a list of science units. The goal is to keep your child asking questions, and to keep feeding them.

Think about how we learn as adults. We go down the rabbit holes that intrigue us, and over time we become experts in what we love. Why should kids be any different?

Charlotte Mason saw the same thing more than a century ago. One chapter of *Home Education* is titled "The Child Gets Knowledge by Means of His Senses." Young children learn by looking, touching and smelling real things first. She also passed along a simple rule for little ones: "Never be within doors when you can rightly be without."

And Scripture sends us outside to learn, too: "But now ask the beasts, and let them teach you; And the birds of the heavens, and let them tell you" (Job 12:7, NASB1995). Every question your child asks about a bug, a leaf or a bird is a chance to show them the One who made it.

## Do I need a curriculum for kindergarten science?

Not to get started. Your child's questions are the curriculum at this age. If you'd like a plan to lean on, I'm a big fan of discovery-led curriculum for science: something built around real things your child can hold, with room to keep going wherever their questions lead.

[Eden's Table](/homeschool) is one place to start. Sprouts, the K-2 year, takes one real plant each week. Your child holds it, smells it, draws it in their notebook and says its name, and the Teacher's Guide gives you the words and the questions so you're never starting from a blank page. It's written for readers and pre-readers together, so a kindergartner who isn't reading yet can sit at the same table as an older sibling. You can read more about that on the [kindergarten page](/homeschool/kindergarten).

## How long should kindergarten science take?

Not long. A short, unhurried lesson is plenty for a five-year-old. When a question grabs them, that's your sign to slow down and follow it.
