Not much. Print the pages and look at the Week at a Glance page first, because that's where the kitchen lab supply list is. For the Sprouts lavender week, the list is dried lavender buds, hot water, a mug, honey, and a cloth square with a tie. That's it.

## How does a week of nature study actually run?

Every day has one job. Here's the Sprouts lavender week, the way it's written:

- **Monday, Read-Aloud and Discussion.** You read "Meet the Family" together and meet Vovó and PopPop on their Tennessee homestead, their family, and Bear the dog. The memory verse is Psalm 139:14, "I am fearfully and wonderfully made; wonderful are Your works" (NASB).
- **Tuesday, Discovery.** Your child rolls a dried bud between two fingers, smells it, feels the leaves, and then draws a lavender spike and labels the flowers and stem. They meet the Latin name too, Lavandula angustifolia.
- **Wednesday, Kitchen Lab.** You make lavender tea together. Measure a teaspoon of buds, a grown-up pours the hot water, you count five minutes of steeping, strain it, stir in a little honey, and taste it once it cools. Then you tie a few buds into a little cloth sachet. In the Notebook your child writes down the color, smell, taste and texture and colors in stars to rate it.
- **Thursday, History and Art.** The Romans scented their baths and laundry with lavender, and its name is traced to the Latin lavare, "to wash." Your child draws lavender in front of Roman arches and finds the Mediterranean and Italy on a map.
- **Friday, Garden and Review.** Go find three living things in a garden, a yard or a windowsill pot, then talk through the week's review questions.

Every day also has a line for Little Sprouts and one for Older Sprouts, so a five-year-old and an eight-year-old can do the same lesson at their own level.

The Seedlings week has the same five-day shape. Monday opens with Psalm 100 read aloud together, and Wednesday is an elderberry syrup lab. Story Seven is the reading that falls in Week 2, so you'll have it ready if you keep going.
