## Part B: teaching style (two-ended, 1-5)

For each line, tap the spot that sounds most like you right now. There are no right answers: every homeschool runs a little differently.

Points: Much more like the left / A bit more like the left / Somewhere in between / A bit more like the right / Much more like the right

Scoring (amends spec section 18 answer 19): each item 1 (left end) to 5 (right end), mean of the 3, scaled to 0-100 where 100 = fully the right end. Each side shows as one of three: leans toward the left end, a balance of both, or leans toward the right end (PROPOSED cut points: 0-30 / 31-69 / 70-100, a wide middle band).

### structure: left = leans toward a steady routine; right = leans toward going with the flow

- **ts-ca1** Most days, I have... LEFT: the energy to teach the way I want | RIGHT: less energy than teaching takes
- **ts-ca2** By early afternoon, I usually... LEFT: still have plenty left | RIGHT: feel worn out

### resistance: left = leans toward finding another way in; right = leans toward steady follow-through

- **ts-re1** When my child pushes back on a lesson, I usually... LEFT: look for another way to teach it | RIGHT: keep the expectation and help them finish
- **ts-re2** Choices about what or how to learn... LEFT: I offer my child often | RIGHT: I mostly make, and explain why
- **ts-re3** When my child is frustrated, I usually... LEFT: pause and talk about how they feel | RIGHT: encourage them to push through

## Research notes

- Why two ends instead of agree/disagree: a large study of survey questions found agree/disagree items gave much lower quality answers than questions with answer choices written for each item (Saris and others, 2010). Two ends also avoid flipped (reversed) items, which can make a short scale less reliable (Suarez-Alvarez and others, Psicothema, 2018).
- Every point on the 5-point line has words, not just the ends: fully labeled scales gave more reliable answers (Menold and others, Field Methods, 2014).
- Both ends are strengths. The follow-through end means clear expectations and encouragement, never pressure: teaching research keeps those apart, and finds that acknowledging a child's frustration and offering choices helps students (Reeve and Cheon, 2021; Joussemet and others, 2008).
- Energy: these lines never use the word burnout and never screen for anything; they only shape the weekly rhythm ideas in your Family Map.
- Honest limits: 3 lines per side is short, so each side reads as a gentle 'leans' in three wide bands, not a score (short scales are less precise: Tavakol and Dennick, 2011).
- Part A: Howard Gardner himself says short questionnaires can't fully capture these strengths and suggests adding views from family (Gardner, 2022). That's why the Family Map also shows each child through more than one person's eyes.
