## Research behind it

- Timing: the strongest test I found (Li and others, Journal of Marketing, 2021: 33,234 shoppers, each send time checked against a group that got no email) found reminders in the first hour LOWERED purchases (30 minutes: 13.1% bought vs 15.2% with no email), 3 to 9 hours made no difference, and 24 hours helped (8.3% vs 6.0%). Those reminders had no discount, like ours.
- Plain subject that names what she was buying: in Rejoiner's review of 7.1 million cart emails, plain branded subject lines sold the most, even with fewer opens.
- From you, mostly text, one button: HubSpot's large email data found plain emails got more clicks than designed ones, and a person's name as the sender tends to get opened more (one SendGrid test: 10% more opens).
- Answers the usual reasons people leave: about 70% of online carts are left behind (Baymard Institute, 50 studies), mostly over cost, trust and not knowing what they get. So the email states the full price, what's inside, and how it arrives.
- No discount, no countdown: your rule, and the FTC calls fake deadlines a dark pattern. The 30-day link is real (Stripe's recovery link).
- Honest caveat: one smaller study (Gopalakrishnan and Park, 2021, text messages sent 4 to 28 days later, not peer reviewed) found a reminder alone sold nothing extra. That's exactly why half the moms get no email and we count purchases, not opens.
- Legal: only moms who said yes get it, with your mailing address and an unsubscribe link (FTC CAN-SPAM guide). No 'ad' label is needed because she said yes first (15 U.S.C. 7704(a)(5)(B)).
