## Storyteller

**Bible example:** Luke, who set out "to write unto thee in order" (Luke 1:3).

**Short version**
Your child comes alive around words right now. Stories, questions, made-up songs, the play-by-play of their whole day at bedtime. Storytellers love being read to, reading on their own, and playing with how words sound. Like Luke, who carefully wrote down the story of Jesus in order, they love putting things into words.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones ask for the same book again and again, narrate their play out loud, and make up rhymes. Older kids read under the covers, write stories nobody assigned, love word games, and can argue a point for a very long time. Teens journal, debate, write, and notice exactly how people say things.

*How to steward it.* Keep reading aloud long after they can read alone. Ask them to tell the story back to you in their own words. Hand them a notebook that is just theirs. Let them read to a younger sibling. Give them Scripture to copy and memorize, because words they love will stay with them.

*The Bible example.* Luke "had perfect understanding of all things from the very first" and wrote it all down in order so Theophilus could know the truth (Luke 1:3-4). A Storyteller's gift is for telling the truth well, and telling it so others can follow.

*In Gardner's terms:* linguistic.

## Organizer

**Bible example:** Joseph, who stored grain in Egypt "as the sand of the sea" (Genesis 41).

**Short version**
Your child loves patterns, order and figuring out how things work right now. Organizers count, sort, line things up, ask "why?" and want the rules of every game. They often enjoy numbers, puzzles and plans. Like Joseph, who planned seven years ahead and stored up grain for a whole nation, they love making sense of things and putting them in order.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones sort toys by color or size, count everything, and notice when the routine changes. Older kids love puzzles, logic games, building systems, and catching mistakes (including yours). Teens enjoy budgets, schedules, strategy games, coding, and any problem with a clear answer.

*How to steward it.* Give them real jobs that need order: the pantry, the chore chart, the grocery budget. Let them see the "why" behind a math rule. Puzzles, strategy games and simple experiments with a question to test are all good fits. Let them plan part of the school week.

*The Bible example.* Joseph saw what was coming, made a plan, and "gathered corn as the sand of the sea, very much, until he left numbering" (Genesis 41:49). His careful order fed his own family in the famine. An Organizer's gift is for bringing order that serves people.

*In Gardner's terms:* logical-mathematical.

## Designer

**Bible example:** Noah, who built the ark to a precise plan (Genesis 6:14-16).

**Short version**
Your child sees the world in pictures and shapes right now. Designers draw, build, map, rearrange the furniture in their heads, and remember where everything is. They often love blocks, maps, art and anything they can picture. Like Noah, who built the ark exactly to the size and shape God gave him, they love seeing how things fit together.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones build tall towers, draw constantly, and can find a lost toy in a messy room. Older kids love maps, diagrams, Lego instructions, and drawing in the margins of every page. Teens enjoy design, photography, layout, architecture, and fixing how a room or a page looks.

*How to steward it.* Let them draw what they learn: a timeline, a map, a labeled diagram. Keep blocks, graph paper and art supplies within reach. Show them the picture before the words when you introduce something new. Let them design something real, like a garden bed or a shelf.

*The Bible example.* God gave Noah exact measurements: "The length of the ark shall be three hundred cubits, the breadth of it fifty cubits, and the height of it thirty cubits" (Genesis 6:15). Noah built it, rooms, window, door and three stories. A Designer's gift is for seeing how a plan comes together.

*In Gardner's terms:* spatial.

## Maker

**Bible example:** Bezalel, filled with wisdom "in all manner of workmanship" (Exodus 31:1-5).

**Short version**
Your child thinks with their hands and their whole body right now. Makers build, take apart, climb, cook, sew and fix. They want to do it, not just watch it. Like Bezalel, whom God filled with skill to work in gold, stone and wood for the tabernacle, they love making real things.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones are always moving, love play dough and kitchen helping, and learn a new skill by trying it over and over. Older kids take things apart, build forts, invent, and get antsy in a chair. Teens love woodworking, cooking, sports, sewing, mechanics, and any project they can hold when it is done.

*How to steward it.* Let them do the lesson, not only hear it: measure in the kitchen, plant the seed, build the model. Many Makers enjoy short lessons with movement in between. Give them real tools and real responsibility. Count finished projects as real schoolwork.

*The Bible example.* God said of Bezalel, "I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship" (Exodus 31:3). Skill with the hands was a gift God gave on purpose, for His house. A Maker's gift is holy work too.

*In Gardner's terms:* bodily-kinesthetic.

## Psalmist

**Bible example:** David, whose harp refreshed King Saul (1 Samuel 16:23).

**Short version**
Your child is tuned to music and rhythm right now. Psalmists hum, drum on the table, make up songs, and remember anything set to a tune. They notice sounds other people miss. Like David, whose playing refreshed King Saul, they love music and the way it moves the heart.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones sing all day, dance to anything, and learn their address from a song. Older kids tap rhythms, pick out tunes by ear, and remember song lyrics after one listen. Teens play an instrument, write songs, make playlists for every mood, and feel music deeply.

*How to steward it.* Set memory work to music: Scripture, skip counting, the books of the Bible. Keep worship music in the home. Let them learn an instrument if you can. Ask them to make up a song about what they just learned.

*The Bible example.* "David took an harp, and played with his hand: so Saul was refreshed, and was well" (1 Samuel 16:23). David went on to write psalms the church still sings. A Psalmist's gift is for worship and for comfort.

*In Gardner's terms:* musical.

## Connector

**Bible example:** Barnabas, "the son of consolation" (Acts 4:36).

**Short version**
Your child is drawn to people right now. Connectors notice how others feel, make friends easily, and want to do things together. They often lead games, settle arguments, and love a group. Like Barnabas, whose name means the son of encouragement, they love building people up.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones notice when someone is sad and bring them a toy. Older kids organize games, love co-op days, and love working with a buddy. Teens are the friend everyone calls, love group projects, and care a lot about fairness and belonging.

*How to steward it.* Let them learn alongside others sometimes: a sibling, a co-op, a study partner. Give them chances to teach, serve and encourage. Talk through what people in stories feel and why. Teach them to listen as well as lead.

*The Bible example.* The apostles named him Barnabas, "which is, being interpreted, The son of consolation" (Acts 4:36). He vouched for Paul when others were afraid of him and stood by John Mark when Paul would not. A Connector's gift is for encouraging people and drawing them in.

*In Gardner's terms:* interpersonal.

## Ponderer

**Bible example:** Mary, who "kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart" (Luke 2:19).

**Short version**
Your child has a rich inner world right now. Ponderers think deeply, need quiet, and know their own mind. They may not say much at first, but what they say is thought through. Like Mary, who kept everything that happened and pondered it in her heart, they love time to think.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones play happily alone and ask a deep question out of nowhere at dinner. Older kids like to work by themselves, keep journals, and need time before they answer. Teens think about who they are, set their own goals, and may need space after a busy day.

*How to steward it.* Give them quiet time and room to think before you ask for an answer. Let them set a goal and track it. Journaling and prayer journals are a good fit. Ask "what do you think?" and wait.

*The Bible example.* After the shepherds came, "Mary kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart" (Luke 2:19). She held what God was doing and thought about it for years. A Ponderer's gift is for reflection and a steady heart.

*In Gardner's terms:* intrapersonal.

## Cultivator

**Bible examples:** Solomon, who "spake of trees" and "of beasts, and of fowl" (1 Kings 4:33), and Adam, who named every living creature (Genesis 2:19).

**Short version**
Your child loves God's creation right now. Cultivators notice birds, bugs, plants and weather, collect rocks and leaves, and want to know what everything is called. They are happiest outside. Like Adam, who named the animals, and Solomon, who spoke of everything from cedars to hyssop, they love knowing and caring for living things.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones bring you every bug and flower and want to know its name. Older kids keep collections, care for pets and gardens, and notice the seasons change before you do. Teens love animals, gardening, hiking, science, and taking care of the land.

*How to steward it.* Take school outside whenever you can. Give them a nature notebook, a garden bed, a pet to care for. Let them learn the names of the plants and birds near your home. Connect what they love to the Creator who made it.

*The Bible examples.* "Whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof" (Genesis 2:19). Solomon "spake of trees, from the cedar tree that is in Lebanon even unto the hyssop that springeth out of the wall" (1 Kings 4:33). A Cultivator's gift is for knowing and keeping God's creation, the first work God gave people.

*In Gardner's terms:* naturalist.

## Seeker

**Bible example:** Samuel, who answered, "Speak; for thy servant heareth" (1 Samuel 3:10).

**Short version**
Your child asks the big questions right now. Seekers wonder about God, heaven, why we are here, and what is true. Their questions can stop you in your tracks. Like young Samuel, who answered God's call with "Speak; for thy servant heareth," they want to know and listen.

**Longer version**
*What you might notice.* Little ones ask where God lives and what heaven is like. Older kids ask why bad things happen and how we know the Bible is true. Teens think about purpose, calling, and what they believe for themselves.

*How to steward it.* Welcome the questions, even the hard ones. Read the Bible together and let them ask. Share how God has answered your own questions. Give them biographies of believers and time to talk about faith, not just facts.

*The Bible example.* When God called in the night, young Samuel answered, "Speak; for thy servant heareth" (1 Samuel 3:10). A Seeker's gift is for listening for God and asking the questions that lead to Him.

*In Gardner's terms:* existential. Gardner himself treated this one as only partly meeting his test for a full intelligence, so we show it the same way: a real strength we see in some children, described honestly.
